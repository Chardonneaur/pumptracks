What the town says about it

A third source, neither the ministry census nor OpenStreetMap: what the town itself publishes. It is the only one that says where a facility came from, who built it and what it cost.

This pumptrack did not come out of a facilities plan: it was initiated by the town's children's council. Families and childminders then chose the playground equipment installed around it. It is the first piece of a larger leisure area laid out along the Ognon river, behind the sports complex.1

The machines arrived in late February 2025, the track was finished before the end of April, and the facility opened on 13 June. Its design and construction went to HTracks, a company whose staff ride BMX themselves; the landscaping firm Effivert took over the site afterwards for the paths and planting. The two loops are not a stylistic choice: they are what allows the track to meet safety regulations.1, 2

The whole leisure area — pumptrack, paths, rope course, playhouse, swing, tables and benches — accounts for €355,000 in the town's 2025 investment budget. The cost of the pumptrack alone is not public: it does not appear in the list of contracts awarded in 2024, and the 2025 list has yet to be published.4, 5

An openly accessible facility still has a keeper: the town closes the leisure area, pumptrack included, for the Saint John's Eve bonfire.3
